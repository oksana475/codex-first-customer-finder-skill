## Core B2B investigation

Establish why the company began looking for new customers at that particular time. Ask for the last concrete episode first and let the interviewee describe it unaided.

Determine who noticed the need, who initiated action, who researched options, who approved resources, and who would use or receive the solution. Do not assume these are the same person.

## Internal trigger hypotheses

Use the following only as an internal post-answer checklist, never as answer choices that could lead the interviewee:

- newly available production capacity;
- a major contract ending;
- reduced volume from a key customer;
- dependence on a small number of customers;
- entry into a new industry;
- new equipment or a new product;
- salespeople relying mainly on the existing customer base;
- existing channels no longer producing enough new business;
- a need to test a segment before hiring a salesperson.

First collect the interviewee's sequence. Compare facts with these hypotheses only after the interview.

## B2B outreach module

When the researched product is B2B outreach, establish from actual behavior:

### Current acquisition route

Identify how the company obtained recent customers: referrals, prior customers, tenders, internal sales, cold calls, advertising, exhibitions, partners, or another route. Ask which route produced the last relevant customer and reconstruct the episode rather than accepting a channel list alone.

### Ownership

Establish who actually searches for new customers: owner, commercial director, manager, dedicated SDR, another role, or nobody systematically. Record decision authority and handoffs.

### Failure point

Do not presume the problem is lead volume. Locate the concrete breakdown across targeting, first contact, reply handling, qualification, sales follow-up, or delivery using the described sequence.

### External-contractor trust

Investigate why the interviewee did or did not entrust first contact to an outside party. Establish through real choices:

- required control and message approval;
- reputational concerns explicitly expressed;
- target-company quality requirements;
- lead qualification requirements;
- the point at which a conversation must transfer internally;
- evidence that created or destroyed trust.

Do not label close review as “fear of losing control” unless the interviewee confirms that interpretation.

## Post-interview comparison

Compare the collected facts with the internal hypotheses. For each match or mismatch, cite the supporting behavior and its evidence status. Report an unestablished trigger rather than selecting the most plausible hypothesis.
