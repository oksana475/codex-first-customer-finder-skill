## Evidence statuses

Apply one status to every material conclusion:

- **FACT** — directly supported by a described, concrete past event or action.
- **STRONG INFERENCE** — supported by several independent facts in the interview.
- **HYPOTHESIS** — plausible interpretation without enough confirmation.
- **NEEDS VERIFICATION** — evidence is missing or contradictory.

Declarations about preferences and future intent are not behavioral facts. A quotation is a fact that the person said something, not proof that its content reflects their behavior.

## Single-interview report

### Context

Record only supplied information: interviewee, role, company/project type, and researched task. Write “not established” for missing fields.

### Facts

List concrete events, actions, decisions, amounts, durations, participants, and results without interpretation. Attach **FACT** to each.

### Trigger

Answer: What happened immediately before active solution-seeking began?

If absent, write:

> Триггер не установлен. Требуется дополнительное уточнение.

### Job To Be Done

Express the desired change in circumstances, not the purchase category. For example: “replace expiring order volume before capacity becomes idle,” not “buy marketing.” Label it **HYPOTHESIS** when the evidence is insufficient.

### What they tried

For each alternative use:

**Action → why chosen → actual result → why continued/stopped**

Include self-service, staff, contractors, other channels/products, the old method, and inaction when evidenced.

### Choice criteria

Separate:

- mandatory criteria;
- desirable criteria;
- trust factors;
- stop factors.

Do not convert generic stated preferences into proven criteria unless a real decision supports them.

### Risks and fears

Include only risks explicitly named or clearly supported by described behavior. Label any interpretation **HYPOTHESIS**.

### Value

Answer: What result does the person consider worth paying for? Describe the outcome rather than the product.

### Customer language

Include the strongest verbatim phrases for offers, email, landing pages, content, or sales. Preserve wording and mark omissions. If no transcript quotations exist, state that verbatim language is unavailable.

### Missing evidence

List missing quality-gate items and one neutral follow-up question for each.

## Contradiction format

**Observation:** The person said X but did Y.

**Possible explanations:** List only as hypotheses, or state that the explanation is not established.

**What to clarify:** Ask one neutral question tied to the discrepancy.

Examples worth examining include claimed urgency with months of inaction, claimed price indifference with lowest-price selection, a wish to delegate with continued hands-on control, demand for more leads while existing inquiries go unanswered, or a claimed critical need without allocated budget. These are prompts for inquiry, not diagnoses.

## Multi-interview synthesis

Analyze recurring patterns separately under:

- situations before solution-seeking;
- triggering events;
- Jobs To Be Done;
- attempted alternatives;
- purchase criteria and valued results;
- barriers and risks;
- repeated customer language;
- behavioral differences between groups.

Then produce:

| Situation-based segment | Trigger | Job To Be Done | What they tried | What failed | Valued result | Main risk | Strong phrases | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Do not call a pattern repeated without naming the numerator and denominator of independent interviews, such as “4 of 7 interviews.” Do not imply statistical representativeness.

### Strongest segments

For each segment state:

- why it is distinct;
- how many interviews support it;
- repeated trigger;
- repeated job;
- repeated value;
- contradictory cases; and
- evidence still needed.

## Situation-based segmentation

Prefer a segment that combines context, change, and task:

- Weak: “manufacturing companies.”
- Strong: “manufacturers expecting idle capacity within one to two months after a major contract ends.”

Industry may be a filter but need not be the defining variable. Keep groups separate when their behavior, trigger, job, or buying logic differs.
